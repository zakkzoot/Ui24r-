# Boho Refresh — Costed Plan (Max-ROI Lens)

**Direction:** the warm **boho / industrial-boho** look from your own renders —
light wood floors, rattan pendants, jute rugs, plants, a brick feature wall,
wood-refaced kitchen bar, and jewel/earth accents (teal, mustard, terracotta).

**Lens:** every line below is judged on **rent uplift per dollar**, not on looks
alone. Items are sorted **Do-now → Worth-it → Skip/Defer**. The goal is to buy
~90% of the "wow" for a fraction of a full fit-out, and to keep as much spend
**portable** (moves to the next unit) as possible.

> **Costs are indicative Phnom Penh USD**, local sourcing, furnish-grade (not
> luxury). They scale with area — see assumptions at the end and adjust by m².

## Phase 1 — Soft boho (DO NOW, ~$650–950)

Cheapest, fastest, mostly **portable**, and it alone makes the place photograph
like the renders. This is where the rent uplift actually comes from.

| Element | What to buy | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Pendant lighting** | Rattan/cane pendants — cluster over bed, single over bar/table | $80–180 | Swap existing fittings; single biggest "boho" signal |
| **Rugs** | Large jute/wool rugs — living zone + each bedroom + a runner | $130–260 | Warms the grey tile *now*, defines zones, portable |
| **Plants** | Monstera, palm, fiddle-leaf, snake plant, pampas + woven/terracotta pots | $90–160 | High impact per dollar; the renders lean heavily on greenery |
| **Curtains** | Teal velvet / mustard linen on bedrooms; light linen elsewhere | $120–220 | The teal drape is the bedroom hero in your render |
| **Bedding & textiles** | Layered linen, mustard throw, jewel/earth cushions, woven blanket | $130–230 | Hotel-plus styling; reads premium in photos |
| **Wall décor** | Woven wall discs, gilt mirror, framed botanical/leaf prints | $70–140 | Fills the big blank walls; mirrors enlarge & brighten |
| **Lighting temp** | Warm-white **2700K** bulbs throughout + 1–2 rattan floor lamps | $50–110 | Kills the clinical feel; the cosy glow is half the mood |
| **Styling props** | Baskets, ceramics, books, tray, pampas stems | $40–80 | Final 10% that makes photos look "designed" |

**Why it's top ROI:** one-off, no trades, no downtime, done in a weekend, and
most of it leaves with you. This is what lets the unit **faster** and supports a
higher asking rent on its own.

## Phase 2 — Light works (WORTH IT, ~$1,300–2,300)

Minor works, no structural change, but this is the **permanent** uplift that
turns a "nicely styled rental" into "renovated apartment" at listing time.

### 2a. Wood-look flooring over the grey tile — **the #1 transformer**
- **SPC / click laminate laid over existing tile** (no demolition = minor work).
- Cost: **~$12–18/m² installed** (budget SPC). Living + 2 bedrooms ≈ 55–70 m² →
  **~$750–1,200**.
- ROI verdict: **highest-impact permanent change.** Grey tile → warm wood is the
  difference between your renders and the original. If budget is tight, **large
  rugs (Phase 1) get you ~60% of the effect for ~10% of the cost** — do floors
  only if you're holding the unit a while.

### 2b. Wood-refaced kitchen bar — *as in your kitchen render*
- New **timber doors/fronts on the existing granite peninsula + base units**
  (local carpenter, plywood + veneer/oak-look). Keep the granite top.
- Cost: **~$250–600** depending on door count.
- ROI verdict: **worth it** — kitchens move rentals, and refacing is a fraction
  of a new kitchen. Pair with the rattan pendant + a styled bar.

### 2c. One brick-slip feature wall — *the industrial-boho nook*
- **Brick slips / veneer tiles** on a single wall (TV wall or the office nook).
- Cost: brick slips **~$8–15/m²** + adhesive + labour → **~$150–350** for one wall.
- ROI verdict: **worth it as ONE accent only.** Huge photo "wow" per dollar; do
  not brick multiple walls (diminishing returns, can read busy).

## Phase 3 — Bathroom & built-ins (SKIP / DEFER for ROI, ~$500–900)

| Element | Cost | Why defer |
|---|---:|---|
| Glass walk-in shower screen | $250–500 | Existing bathroom is already stone + rain shower; a screen is *nice*, not rent-moving |
| Wall-hung WC + concealed cistern | $200–400 | Real plumbing work, low incremental rent — keep the existing WC |
| Custom built-in shelving/wardrobes | $300–700+ | Freestanding rattan/wood credenza (Phase 1) gives the look without the cost or the permanence risk |

**Verdict:** the bathroom already photographs well — spend $30 on white towels,
a mat and a plant and move on. Revisit Phase 3 only if you're repositioning the
unit to a clearly higher rent bracket.

## Budget & ROI summary

| Scenario | Spend | What you get |
|---|---:|---|
| **Lean** (Phase 1 only) | **$650–950** | Fully boho-*styled*, portable, weekend job |
| **Recommended** (P1 + floors + kitchen reface) | **$1,800–2,800** | Looks like your renders; permanent uplift; one accent brick wall optional |
| **Full** (P1 + P2 + P3) | **$2,450–4,150** | Everything in the renders incl. bathroom |

**Rent-uplift logic (max-ROI):**
- Phase 1 is near-pure ROI — portable, supports higher rent + faster letting,
  typical payback **well under a year**.
- Phase 2 is the permanent step-change that justifies a higher *bracket*; floors
  + kitchen reface are the two that genuinely move the achievable rent. Even a
  modest **$40–80/month** uplift pays the recommended package back in
  **~2–3 years**, then it's profit plus reduced vacancy.
- Phase 3 is where cost-per-extra-dollar-of-rent falls off — hence "skip/defer."

**Keep it low-risk:** prioritise **portable** spend (Phase 1) and **reversible,
no-demolition** works (SPC over tile, refacing not replacing, slips not
structural brick). Nothing here needs permits or changes the building.
